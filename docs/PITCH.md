## The script (about 3 minutes)

**1. The person ([Speaker 1])**

[Ma'am/Sir ___] is the OJT coordinator at [school]. Every term, hundreds
of students leave for on-the-job training, and this one person must know
where each student is and how many hours they have done. [Last ___, they
needed to ___, so they ___. Use the real story from the conversation.]

**2. Today ([Speaker 1])**

Right now it lives on paper and in spreadsheets. Students hand in daily
time records. The coordinator types them in, checks them, and adds them
up by hand. When a student finishes, someone builds that student's hours
summary from scratch. [One real number: it takes ___ minutes per
student, ___ hours a week.]

**3. Our fix ([Speaker 2])**

Dugtong is a web app where the coordinator uploads the student list,
students log their own daily hours, the coordinator approves them, and
one click produces the hours report. The coordinator stops typing in
other people's hours. They only check and approve.

**4. The demo moment ([Speaker 2])**

In Session 3 you will watch this: we add a partner company, upload five
sample students, and one student logs a day's time-in and time-out. The
coordinator approves it, the hour total updates, and we click once to
get the student's hours report.

**5. How we'll know ([Speaker 3])**

Our test is simple. [Coordinator ___] produces a student's hours report
in under [___] minutes, where today it takes [___]. We will bring you
their answer, not ours. Every student in our demo is sample data, and
each student can only ever see their own hours.

## The three hardest questions

**1. "Have you actually watched a coordinator do this?"**
This is our weakest point. Right now, no. Our evidence is the project
proposal, not observation. Honest answer: "Not yet. We're meeting
[coordinator] by [date], and the numbers we show in Session 3 will come
from that conversation." If you can say "yes, we did", the pitch is much
stronger. Meet a coordinator before presenting.

**2. "Why not a shared Google Sheet?"**
A Sheet either lets everyone edit everything or makes the coordinator
type it all. Dugtong lets each student enter only their own hours, locks
approved hours so they can't be changed silently, and builds the report
itself. Be ready to admit that if the coordinator says a Sheet is fine,
we've learned something important.

**3. "The coordinator isn't at the company. How do they know the hours
are real?"**
Fair. The coordinator checks entries against whatever proof they use
today, such as a signed daily time record. We'll confirm what that proof
is in the conversation. Supervisor approval is the obvious next step,
and it's parked in our later list on purpose.
